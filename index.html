Course description and goals

Participants will learn to use the RLMT by taking a real project from start to finish: planning, scheduling, observing and analyzing an exoplanet transit. The course covers the MACRO Consortium and the RLMT, the basics of astronomical imaging, how to schedule with MACRO's system, and how to run MACRO's reduction and photometry pipelines. It is designed to prepare participants to become MACRO Preceptors who will lead the telescope operations and science projects in the next years.

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

  1. Explain what MACRO is, how the RLMT works, and what their roles as a preceptor are.
  2. Describe the sky and decide whether a target can be observed using coordinates, time, altitude and airmass.
  3. Describe how a CCD/CMOS image is formed and how bias, dark and flat frames are used to calibrate it.
  4. Choose a target and set the appropriate exposure, filter and cadence for the RLMT.
  5. Submit observation requests through the MACRO scheduling system and check them.
  6. Plan the schedule for an entire night for the RLMT.
  7. Quickly analyze astronomical data for quality checks
  8. Carefully analyze astronomical data for scientific measurement.
  9. Run the MACRO reduction and photometry pipelines.
  10. Create light curves.
  11. Measure the transit depth and mid-transit time for an exoplanet.
  12. Present their result and explain what makes a transit observation succeed or fail.

Weekly schedule

The course has three parts. Weeks 1–6 cover MACRO, the RLMT, imaging, quick-look data checks and a brief intro to transits; weeks 7 and 9 are for scheduling, which is particularly important (spring break falls in week 8); and weeks 10–14 cover the MACRO pipelines and the final project. This is a training course and you learn to run and check the existing reduction and photometry pipelines, not to build them from scratch (though there is space for that if it is something you are interested in). Exoplanet transits are the worked example throughout.

Final Project: your exoplanet transit

Participants work in pairs. Each pair observes one known exoplanet transit with the RLMT and presents a measured light curve on April 28.

  1. Choose the target (weeks 6–7). Pick a primary and two backups that are visible from Sonoita between late March and mid-April. Each needs the full transit plus at least 30 minutes of baseline before and after, at airmass below about 2. The transit depth should be large enough for a 0.5 m telescope to detect.
  2. Schedule it (weeks 7–9). Write the RLMT requests, including filter, exposure time, and timing window. Have a classmate review them before you submit.
  3. Reduce it (weeks 11–12). Check that the reduction has run properly on your frames, establish final zero points, and build the light curve.
  4. Measure something (week 13). Fit the transit for depth (and Rp/R*) and mid-transit time, then compare them with the published values and the predicted time.
  5. Present it (week 14). Give a 5–7 minute talk covering the target, the observing plan, the light curve, the measured values and what you would change. Optionally, submit your timing to a public database.

Software and resources

  • RLMT and MACRO: the draft MACRO Documentation (in progress) and the MACRO scheduling system (accounts set up in week 1)
  • Image viewing: SAOImage DS9 for inspecting frames
  • PhotStars: For performing photometry on images
  • Transit planning: a transit prediction tool, such as the NASA Exoplanet Archive transit service or the Swarthmore transit finder
  • Python and VS Code: Python through conda (Miniforge), using the course photometry environment file, and VS Code as the editor, with its Python and Jupyter extensions. Set both up in week 1, on your own computer, following the step-by-step Python setup guide. Plan to keep ~10 GB of free disk space for RLMT data.
